Quick math:

Assume your an AI lab and you got a ~1.8T param MoE model, 16 experts × ~111B params, with 2 experts active per token = ~280B active params.

If you serve at FP8 (1 byte/param), you can estimate theoretical max token throughput just from HBM bandwidth: Max tokens/sec = BW / (2 × active bytes)

H100: 3.35e12 / (2 × 300e9) ≈ 5.6 tok/s × ~0.3 efficiency ≈ 1.7 tok/s per GPU.

With 28 way sharding: 28 × 3.35 = 94 TB/s = ~47 tok/s, ~15–20 realistically.

With batching a pod does ~3,000 tok/s. At 30 tok/s per user, that’s ~100 concurrent users per pod. Anthropic charges $25/M output and $5/M input tokens on Opus 4.8, so revenue is output dominated.

H100 pod (~28 GPUs @ $4/hr) = ~$112/hr. Producing 3,000 tok/s × 3,600 sec = 10.8M output tokens/hr. At $25/M output tokens that’s roughly $270/hr revenue.

Profits per H100 pod: $270 rev − $112 cost = ~$158/hr, ~59% gross margin.

For B200 (8 TB/s HBM, ~2.4× the bandwidth, ~$6/hr). A 15 GPU pod does ~5,500 tok/s batched = ~180 users. That’s 19.8M tok/hr × $25/M ≈ $495/hr revenue at ~$90/hr cost.

Profit for B200 pod: $495 rev − $90 cost = ~$405/hr profit, or ~82% gross margin.

A pod costs about the same whether you are serving 1 user or 180 and every marginal user is pure margin until the KV cache fills.

Now flip to the neocloud side that rents to the AI lab. What does it cost to OWN the metal?

For the H100 pod @ ~$30K/GPU + ~40% for server/networking/cooling ≈ ~$1.2M capex.

Power is about 700W per GPU and if we assume $0.07/kWh using Texas/Louisiana pricing we get:

700w × 28 × 1.3 PUE ≈ 25.5 kW = ~$1.3K/mo

For a B200 pod: ~$45K/GPU + 40% ≈ ~$945K capex. Power: 1,000W × 15 × 1.3 ≈ 19.5 kW = ~$1K/mo.

Break even rate on renting GPUs assuming 24/7 uptime & ~70% utilization:

H100: $4 × 28 × 0.7 × 720hr ≈ $56K/mo − power ≈ $55K net. Which is roughly ~22 months to break even.

B200: $6 × 15 × 0.7 × 720hr ≈ $45K/mo − power ≈ $44K net. ~21 months to break even.

Both ~2 year payback, then ~$45–55K/month in pure margin per pod. Beautiful 😍

So therefore Neoclouds renting at $4–6/GPU-hr, get ~40–60% gross after capex + power. The model layer that rents FROM the neocloud, then turns the $90–112/hr cost into $270–495/hr of API revenue giving ~60–82% gross.

The token layer captures the fatter margin. And neocloud eats capex risk + depreciation; but at the end of the day the model provider holds all the pricing power as without good utilization neoclouds can’t make money and without high token costs margins collapse and put pressure on Neoclouds to drive prices down.

All of this assumes you paid cash for the GPUs but almost nobody does.

Neoclouds borrow heavily to buy GPUs and pledge the GPUs themselves as collateral. And that changes the picture two ways:

  1. Debt service. At ~10–15% financing, a $1.2M H100 pod carries ~$10–15K/mo in interest before you serve a single token. That eats roughly a third of your net and pushes break even math from ~2 years past 3.

  2. Depreciation is even worse Loans amortize on an assumed 5–6 yr useful life but H100 rental have fallen from ~$8/hr to under $3/hr in only two years. If the collateral halves in its economic value while the loan is still on a 6-yr schedule, you’re underwater because the asset is depreciating faster than the debt.

Now the most dangerous part here is unlike regular cloud hosting this is a treadmill. Every new generation of chips makes the last one’s economics worse, meaning you have to keep borrowing to stay competitive:

Say you bought all those h100 pods and Rubin’s land with a 2 or 4x TCO efficiency gain, the collateral backing your loan is worth maybe half what you paid while you still owe most of the principal.

To stay in the neocloud game you need to take on MORE debt to buy Rubin pods. So now you’re servicing old debt on H100s that are earning less and worth less new debt on Rubin pods that themselves start depreciating the day Feynman is announced.

New GPU generations roughly coming every 2 years if stack this math across 2–3 generations the debt servicing compounds: each cycle you’re carrying a layer of underwater legacy inventory + fresh leverage on the newest tier. Depreciation isn’t a one-time hit either it’s a step down that repeats every ~18 to 24 months, and the debt from each cycle outlives the earning power of the hardware it bought.

The math only works if demand + rental rates keep rising fast enough to outrun the depreciation curve. The moment demand blinks the utilization dips or rates compress you’ve got fixed debt payments on a stack of assets losing value faster than you can pay them down. That’s how a an amazing “80% margin” business becomes insolvent without revenue ever going to zero.

Light-asset model layer holds pricing power more but have their own risks. It’s clear however that the neocloud layer is on a never ending treadmill and will probably get squeezed first when push comes to shove.

Additionally, this all assumes Nvidia still has the best hardware in 5 years for inference which is a big assumption. TPU, Tranium, or ASIC-X takes off and the inventory as well as data center design needs to be completely gutted.
